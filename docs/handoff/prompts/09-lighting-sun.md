# 09 — Lighting for the RTX 3060 and a realistic Sun

Goal: realistic lighting inside and outside, using everything the browser/GPU allows (WebGL2 now; document a WebGPU path). Complaints: unrealistic lighting inside and outside; the Sun does not look real.
Do: physically based units (sun illuminance ~128 klux, exposure like an ISS camera; EV-based auto-exposure with eye adaptation), AgX or ACES-fitted tone mapping, TAA (temporal anti-aliasing) or SMAA+, cascaded or camera-fitted soft shadows (PCSS) with contact hardening, GTAO-quality AO (keep the cabin-scale kernel inside), screen-space reflections for metals/glass, RectAreaLight (LTC) for LED panels inside, light probes/irradiance volumes or baked lightmaps for interiors (bake offline with a path tracer such as three-gpu-pathtracer on the GPU), earthshine as a large blue-white area light from below, sunlight shafts through windows (volumetric in cabins with dust), correct night/eclipse transitions; the Sun: correct angular size (0.53°), limb darkening, very high luminance with bloom/glare that is physically plausible (diffraction spikes optional, no cartoon flare), corona only during occultation by Earth's limb, sunrise/sunset colouring through the atmosphere; check the lens flare and exposure in every view.
Acceptance: screenshots (lab, cupola, ring deck, airlock, EVA sunlit/shadow/eclipse, sunrise over the limb) compared with ISS photos; post + shadows <= 4 ms GPU at 1080p; write docs/agent-tasks/webgpu-path.md (what would improve with WebGPU, effort).
